Why ClimbQuest Matters

ClimbQuest was motivated by a recurring problem in indoor climbing: many climbers do not only need more routes 鈥?they need clearer guidance, easier route recording, and more engaging ways to continue their climbing journey.

Problem Space: Route Choice Is Not Always Obvious

Indoor climbers often rely on wall labels, memory, or informal advice to decide what to climb next. For beginners and low-frequency climbers, route difficulty labels can feel technical, inconsistent, or hard to connect with their own ability. This creates uncertainty when choosing routes and can reduce motivation after a failed attempt or a long break.

What Is Missing in Current Climbing Support

Gap 1 鈥?Route labels do not explain personal fit

Existing route labels usually show difficulty, but they do not explain whether a route fits a climber鈥檚 current confidence, style, or goal.

Gap 2 鈥?Recording routes is still inconvenient

Many climbers rely on memory, photos, or notes. This makes it hard to revisit attempts, compare progress, or share routes clearly.

Gap 3 鈥?Social motivation is under-supported

Climbers may want to discover what others are trying, but there is often no lightweight platform for sharing custom routes, comments, or route attempts.

Human-Centred and Playful Value

Personal guidance, not generic difficulty

ClimbQuest uses quiz-based route matching to make route choice feel more personal and less intimidating.

Creation, not just consumption

The DIY route tool lets users become route creators, not only route followers.

Community, not isolated attempts

The community feature turns routes into shareable challenges that can be liked, commented on, and tried by others.

For ClimbQuest, playfulness is not only about visual decoration. It comes from giving climbers a sense of choice, creativity, and social participation.

Project Aim and Objectives

Aim: To design and implement a mobile-first climbing route platform that helps indoor climbers discover suitable routes, create custom routes, and share route experiences with others.

Objectives:

  • Help users find routes that match their ability, preference, and goals.
  • Provide a visual DIY editor for creating custom climbing routes.
  • Support route sharing, likes, comments, and trying community routes.
  • Make the experience playful, lightweight, and suitable for mobile use inside climbing gyms.
  • Evaluate whether ClimbQuest improves personalization, usability, engagement, and adoption intention.
