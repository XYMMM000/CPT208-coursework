Crazy Eights

In the early ideation stage, we used the Crazy Eights method to quickly explore multiple design directions for ClimbQuest. Each sketch focused on one or more user needs identified from our research: reducing route-selection uncertainty, increasing creative ownership, and supporting motivation through social interaction.

Why We Chose ClimbQuest

We selected Concept C: ClimbQuest because it best integrates the three main user needs identified in our research.

First, users often struggle with route selection. They may not know which route matches their skill level, confidence, preferred style, or training goal. The Personalized Route Recommendation feature addresses this by using a playful MBTI-style questionnaire to guide users toward suitable routes.

Second, users want more autonomy and creative involvement. Instead of only completing routes designed by others, some users want to design challenges themselves. The DIY Route Creation feature allows users to select holds on a wall image, create their own route, and define its name, difficulty, style, and description.

Third, users need ongoing motivation and social feedback. Climbing can feel isolated when users do not know what others are trying or when their progress ends after a single session. The Community Sharing & Interaction feature allows users to upload routes, browse routes created by others, like, comment, and try community-shared routes.

Compared with the other two alternatives, ClimbQuest offers the strongest balance between feasibility, creativity, and human-centred value. It does not only recommend routes; it creates a full route experience loop:

Discover a route -> Try a route -> Create a route -> Share a route -> Receive feedback -> Try again

This makes ClimbQuest more playful, participatory, and socially engaging than a single-function route tool.

Low-Fidelity Prototype

Our low-fidelity prototype focused on a mobile-first experience with three core user flows.

Low-Fidelity Prototype Overview

This overview shows the early mobile wireframes used to structure ClimbQuest's core user flows before moving into high-fidelity design.

Flow 1: Personalized Route Recommendation

Users complete a playful MBTI-style quiz and receive route suggestions based on experience, preferences, confidence, and goals. This reduces route-selection pressure before climbing.

Flow 2: DIY Route Creation

Users select holds on a wall image, preview the route, and add name, difficulty, style, and description. This supports creative ownership.

Flow 3: Community Sharing & Interaction

Users browse, like, comment on, upload, and try shared routes. This extends motivation beyond a single climbing session.

Prototype Focus:
  • clear navigation between the three main features
  • simple mobile interactions
  • minimal text
  • fast route discovery and creation
  • visible community feedback actions such as like, comment, and try
